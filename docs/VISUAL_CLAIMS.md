# Visual claims — analog hillside rescue

These claims were fixed before production asset generation. Critic rounds may reject both a frame and a metric. Reference comparisons use the same aspect ratio; motion captures, not parked shots, decide the result.

1. **Hero recognition.** The red player coupe is the first readable object and occupies 30–38% of landscape frame height at normal chase distance. Its wheel arches, cabin, rear mass, glass and tyres remain separable in shade.
   - Gameable by enlarging an ugly object or adding an outline. A critic must reject weak form/materials even if the box measurement passes.
2. **Vehicle form.** No car reads as stacked boxes with wheels. Each has continuous body volume, four plausible wheel openings, appropriate overhangs and a distinct greenhouse/rear silhouette.
   - Gameable by excessive smoothing. Believable structure and stance still matter.
3. **Rival distinction.** In desaturated silhouettes and without labels, Charger, Technician and Defender can be matched across two motion frames by body shape; in colour they are additionally ochre, faded blue and forest green.
   - Gameable by extreme cartoon proportions. All four must still belong to one racing category.
4. **Track readability.** Both road edges and the next corner direction are identifiable in at least 90% of motion frames. Asphalt remains separated from dirt/grass in sun and shade.
   - Gameable by oversized luminous lines. Only physical paint, shoulders and track furniture count.
5. **Depth.** Every representative racing frame contains foreground parallax, midground track furniture/vehicles and a background terrain layer; no frame is empty sky plus flat ground.
   - Gameable by randomly scattered clutter. Placement must respond to the road and corner.
6. **Warm/cool separation.** Representative frames contain warm direct sunlight and cooler readable shade without crushing asphalt, tyres or car cabins to featureless black.
   - Gameable by split-toning the entire image. The relationship must arise from lighting and material response.
7. **No robotic residue.** No major frame contains cyan/purple glow, sci-fi pylons, floating road pieces, metallic laboratory massing, holographic panels or robot-like figures.
   - This is a hard eye check with zero allowed exceptions.
8. **Authored asymmetry.** Each major corner has one recognisable asymmetric landmark or spectator cluster, and repeated props do not form perfect mirrored rows or identical spacing.
   - Gameable by random transforms alone. Clusters need compositional intent.
9. **Road surface quality.** Asphalt shows restrained aggregate/wear/roughness variation without obvious tiling, mirror response or uniform flat charcoal.
   - Gameable by noisy high-frequency maps. Variation must survive motion without shimmer.
10. **Motion quality.** At least one close-racing, overtake, fast-corner, shaded and finish frame looks intentionally art-directed while moving. Cars remain readable without full-frame blur.
    - Decided only by round-3 motion critique.
11. **AI readability.** Motion frames and telemetry show the Charger attempting smaller gaps/late entries, the Technician following a smoother optimal line, and the Defender moving once to cover then returning. Labels alone do not satisfy this.
    - Gameplay evidence and frames must agree.
12. **Analog HUD.** UI reads as warm cream/charcoal motorsport timing graphics and never as glass, neon, corner brackets or a sci-fi dashboard. World remains the visual focus.
    - Gameable by recolouring the old layout. Hierarchy and information density are judged too.
13. **Phone survival.** At 390×844 and 844×390, car, road direction, position/lap, accelerator, brake and handbrake remain legible; touch controls do not cover the apex.
    - Minimum 52 px targets and real-touch evidence remain binding.
14. **Made-thing test.** With title and HUD context hidden, a moving frame reads within two seconds as a deliberately composed analog hillside racing game, not a generic Three.js or AI-generated prototype.
    - Final blind-pairs verdict; no aggregate score can override it.
